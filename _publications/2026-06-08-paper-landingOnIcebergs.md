Abstract: This article presents a strategy that enables small multirotors to reliably land on steep and irregular ice surfaces, such as those found on icebergs and glaciers. The proposed system integrates three key components: 1) ice-gripping feet equipped with opposing, passively retractable spines that generate anisotropic shear forces for both uphill- and downhill-facing landings; 2) a friction-based dissipative landing gear that absorbs kinetic energy and increases spine penetration by preventing rebound; and 3) reverse thrust (RT) at touchdown to increase normal forces and substantially increase shear forces. A detailed study of ice–spine interaction guided the selection of two distinct sets of spine properties for the downhill and uphill spines, each to meet a distinct function, respectively: 1) aggressive braking on steep ice to avoid holes, crevasses, or ice protrusions, and 2) easy spine re-engagement on steep slopes under low normal loads to prevent slipping after landing. A custom quadrotor drone, the Ice Dart, was designed for high power and maneuverability in strong winds, enabling validation of the strategy on real icebergs and glaciers. Laboratory drop tests of the Ice Dart demonstrated successful landings at descent speeds of 1–3 m/s on ice slopes of up to 60°, while confirming the advantages of the specialized feet. Field trials in Iceland demonstrated the performance on diverse ice types, iceberg sizes and shapes, achieving 24 successful landings on icebergs and glaciers, at inclinations of up to 58° and heading offsets of up to 45°, in winds of up to 30 km/h, and achieving a 100% success rate.
